The basics

13

The settings a team can actually change, and what each one moves on the list from slide 3 (file size, streaming, memory, CPU, platform compliance, sound quality). Codec: the biggest lever, because it changes all of them together. Bitrate or quality: data per second of sound. 1,536 kbps for stereo PCM against roughly 64 to 160 kbps for a perceptual codec; 128 kbps works out to about 1 MB a minute, a ratio of 12:1. Constant or variable: many tools show a "quality" slider instead of a bitrate, which is variable bitrate under the hood. Sample rate: halving it halves the data and the decode work, and removes everything above the new limit, so it suits dull or low sounds. Channels: a mono source needs half of what stereo does. In memory or streamed: not strictly a codec setting, but it sits next to one and decides whether the cost lands on RAM or on the disk. The mental model for the rest of the talk: every codec trades bits for something. The next slide covers the catches that are not settings at all.
