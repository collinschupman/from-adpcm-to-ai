The basics

31

Bink Audio and RAD Audio come from RAD Game Tools, the company behind Bink Video, Oodle and the Miles Sound System, which Epic acquired in January 2021. Both are perceptual codecs designed for games rather than for music distribution, so they aim for perceptual-codec file sizes at close to ADPCM's decode cost. Epic's numbers: Bink Audio is capable of perceptually lossless 10:1 compression with decode speeds closer to ADPCM than MP3 or Vorbis. Unreal's documentation describes RAD Audio as Bink Audio with better quality at comparable CPU usage, and it only accepts 48, 44.1, 32 or 24 kHz. Bink Audio became free in Unreal with UE 4.27 and UE5 Early Access in June 2021, RAD Audio was added after that, and Bink Audio is Unreal's default, so if you are on Unreal you are probably already using it. The real-world example: Epic says Apex Legends, from Titanfall's studio Respawn, plays 80 to 100 voices at once, all in Bink Audio. That is the Titanfall problem from the start of the talk solved a second way; that link is your observation, not Epic's. The catch is that they are proprietary. Inside Unreal that doesn't matter; outside it, check licensing with Epic. These are vendor claims; measure on your own content and hardware.
