The basics

20

This follows straight from the bitrate slide: for perceptual codecs, the ratio is just the bitrate you picked, seen from the size side. Compression ratio is the original size divided by the compressed size, so 4:1 means a quarter of the size. The bars are one minute of stereo at 48 kHz, 16-bit: 48,000 × 16 × 2 = 1,536 kbps, or 11.5 MB a minute, the same figure from the cost-of-PCM slide. FLAC is roughly half, but it depends on the material, usually 1.5:1 to 2:1. ADPCM is a fixed 4:1, so 2.9 MB whatever the sound. Perceptual codecs are different: the ratio is a setting. The two Opus rows are its full range. At its highest setting, 510 kbps, Opus is only about 3:1 (1,536 / 510), 3.8 MB a minute, bigger than ADPCM. At its lowest, 6 kbps, it is 256:1, about 45 KB a minute, but 6 kbps is meant for narrowband speech and would wreck music. Real settings sit in between: the bitrate slide's 64 to 128 kbps for stereo music is 24:1 to 12:1, 0.5 to 1 MB a minute. Halve the bitrate and you double the ratio, until you start to hear it. Point out that the ratio is only one column: ADPCM is four times smaller and nearly free to decode; Opus can be far smaller and costs the most CPU. [The FLAC figure is a general one; check it against your own material before quoting it.]
