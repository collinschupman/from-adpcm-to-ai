How to choose

31

~1.5 min. A caveat on question 4 of the decision order, before anyone moves everything to the platform codec. The myth to bust: hardware decoding is free. It is not, even on current consoles. The decoder is usually a DSP co-processor, and it is slower than the console's main CPU. What it gives you is parallelism: decoding happens alongside the CPU instead of on it. In absolute terms it cannot decode more audio than the CPU could; the win is that the CPU is free for other work, and you can mix hardware and software sounds to use both at once. Latency. There are two ways to use that parallelism, and in Wwise the bLowLatencyHwCodec setting picks one. Same frame (true): the data goes to the decoder, the CPU handles other sounds or yields until the DSP is done, and everything is mixed in the same audio frame, so nothing is out of sync. The catch is that the Wwise profiler counts the waiting time, so audio looks more expensive than it is. Next frame (false): send the data this frame, collect the result next frame. That hides the DSP time completely and is the better choice for overall performance, but every hardware sound is one audio frame late, which matters when hardware and software sounds must line up, or in latency-sensitive games like rhythm games. Hidden costs. They differ by platform, driver and API, which is why these codecs are hard to compare, but the common ones are: commands that cross into the OS kernel; command queues guarded by locks that can stall; copying compressed input, and sometimes the output, to memory the decoder can see; converting the output to the format the rest of the mixer uses; and decoders that only hand results back per batch. Rule of thumb: these fixed costs are paid per sound, so very short sounds, under about 100 ms, can cost more in hardware than in software. Be careful with dry footsteps, impacts, rapid-fire weapons built with Wwise's trigger rate, and granular-style Random or Sequence containers with sample-accurate transitions. How much it matters depends on how often those sounds play, how long they are, and the platform.
