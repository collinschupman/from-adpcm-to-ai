~1 min. Now the two ways to spend a bitrate. Constant bitrate, CBR, gives every second the same budget, whatever is happening, so the size is exactly predictable and every packet is the same size. That is why voice chat and network streaming tend to use it. The catch is that a busy passage gets the same bits as silence, so quality goes up and down with the content. Variable bitrate, VBR, spends more on busy, complex passages and less on quiet or simple ones. At the same average, the dashed line, it sounds better, but the final size depends on the content, so you only know it after encoding. When a tool gives you a quality slider instead of a bitrate, like Vorbis in Wwise, that is VBR under the hood. For game assets on disk, VBR is usually the right default. Only perceptual codecs have this choice. ADPCM and PCM spend the same bits on every sample, so they are always constant.
