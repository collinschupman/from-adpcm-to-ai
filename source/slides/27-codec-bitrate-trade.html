Bitrate: quality for size

Bitrate is how many bits each second of sound gets. It is the main tradeoff you control.

Go lower

Smaller files, less memory and I/O. Then smeared transients, swirly highs, a dull top end.

Go higher

Closer to the original, until it is transparent. Past that, only bigger.

Why it matters

Once the codec is picked, bitrate is the setting you change, sound by sound.

The basics

27

~1 min. This is the most important slide in the basics, so slow down. Bitrate is how many bits each second of sound gets, in kilobits per second, and it is a straight trade: quality for size. The scale is Opus stereo across its whole range, with the size of one minute under each mark. Go lower and the file shrinks, so it costs less disk, memory and streaming bandwidth, but the codec throws away more, and you start to hear it: transients smear, highs go swirly, the top end goes dull. Go higher and it gets closer to the original, until listeners cannot tell the difference, which is called transparent; for Opus stereo music, Xiph puts that around 128 kbps. Past that point you are only paying in size. Why it matters most: the codec decides what kind of loss you get, but once you have picked one, bitrate, or the quality slider that stands in for it, is the setting you will touch again and again, per sound or per sound bank. It sets the file size and the quality at the same time, and with most perceptual codecs it moves the decode cost too. Every other number on the next slides, the ratios and the sizes, follows from it. ADPCM has no bitrate setting; its only levers are sample rate and channel count. Sizes are 48 kHz stereo, one minute, decimal units; the transparency point is from https://wiki.xiph.org/Opus_Recommended_Settings
