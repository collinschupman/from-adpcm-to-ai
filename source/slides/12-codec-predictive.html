ADPCM and its variants

The basics

12

~1.5 min. Predictive codecs, which in games means ADPCM and its variants, work in time, one sample at a time. Walk the diagram: the codec looks at the last couple of samples and guesses the next one, the hollow dot. It compares the guess with the actual sample, the solid dot, and the difference is the error, in red. Only that error is stored, rounded to 4 bits instead of 16. Audio is smooth from one sample to the next, so the guess is usually close and the error is small, which is why 4 bits are enough. The adaptive part of adaptive differential PCM is the step size: it grows when the signal is busy and shrinks when it is quiet, so those 4 bits stretch further. The decoder makes exactly the same guesses and adds the stored error back: a few additions per sample, which is why ADPCM is nearly free to play. What it loses is the fine detail lost in rounding, which you hear as a steady low-level hiss, most on bright, high-frequency sounds. Every sample costs the same 4 bits, so the size is fixed at about 4:1, whatever the sound.
