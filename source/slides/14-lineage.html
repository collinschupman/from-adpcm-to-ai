A short history

1973

ADPCM

Predictive coding from Bell Labs. Still in every engine.

1993

MP3

Perceptual coding goes mainstream.

1997

AAC

MP3's successor, later decoded in phone hardware.

2002

Vorbis

Royalty-free. Becomes the game default.

2005

XMA

Xbox 360's format, decoded in hardware.

2011

ATRAC9

Sony's low-latency codec, from PS Vita on.

2012

Opus

IETF standard. Speech and music in one codec.

Predictive coding first, then perceptual coding, then platform-native codecs, then one flexible open codec.

The past

14

~1.5 min. Don't linger on dates. The arc: predictive coding (ADPCM) → perceptual coding (MP3, AAC) → royalty-free perceptual (Vorbis) → platform-native hardware codecs (XMA, ATRAC9) → a single flexible open codec (Opus). Dates: ADPCM research at Bell Labs early 1970s; MPEG-1 Layer III 1993; AAC 1997; Vorbis 1.0 2002; XMA with Xbox 360 2005; ATRAC9 with PS Vita 2011; Opus as RFC 6716 in 2012.
