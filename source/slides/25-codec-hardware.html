The basics

25

Hardware acceleration is not a general speed-up: each platform has dedicated hardware, or a tuned native decoder, for one specific codec, and only that codec benefits. Xbox Series X|S decodes Opus in hardware through its audio processor, and still supports XMA, the codec Xbox decoded in hardware since the Xbox 360, for older content. Nintendo Switch has a hardware Opus decoder, running libopus on its audio DSP. It is a small pool: one Wwise user hit 'Failed creating Opus decoder' errors and fixed them by keeping Opus for music and large files and using ADPCM for everything else. Switch 2: [fill in from the Nintendo SDK; I have no public source for which codec, if any, it decodes in hardware, so do not guess on stage]. PlayStation uses ATRAC9, Sony's codec optimized for games, through its SDK; what is hardware-decoded on PS4 and PS5 is in Sony's developer documentation, which is under NDA. iPhone: Apple's audio APIs offer a hardware AAC decoder. Android is the reason this row no longer says phones: many Android chips can offload AAC to a DSP for low-power music playback, but games generally decode through the platform's software decoder, so for game audio do not count on hardware AAC on Android. Everything else decodes on the CPU, and hardware decoders are a fixed resource, so plan which sounds get them.
