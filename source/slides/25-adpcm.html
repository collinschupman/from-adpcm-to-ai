The basics

25

This is the predictive family from the last slide, as games actually ship it. Land the meme: every console from the SNES on stored samples as some flavour of ADPCM, and it is still in every engine today. ADPCM stores each sample as a 4-bit correction to a prediction, so it is a fixed 4:1 against 16-bit PCM whatever the sound. Decoding is a few additions per sample, which is why it costs almost nothing, and every block decodes on its own, so loops and seeks land exactly on the sample. The cost is quality: a little hiss, most audible on bright, high-frequency sounds. That makes it the classic choice for short, frequent sounds that play constantly: footsteps, impacts, UI. Fun aside: ADPCM is also alive in emulation. A PlayStation emulator has to re-implement the console's sound chip, ADPCM decoder included, to play the original game data; jsgroth's blog post on the PS1 SPU walks through it: https://jsgroth.dev/blog/posts/ps1-spu-part-1/ [Skim the post before presenting so you can describe what it covers.]
