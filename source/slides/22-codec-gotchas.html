Other gotchas

Size and CPU are not the whole story. Four more things can rule a codec out.

Looping

Some codecs add a sliver of silence at the start and end of a file. A seamless loop clicks or gaps unless your tool trims it. MP3 is the classic case.

Licensing

Some codecs are covered by patents. Vorbis and Opus are royalty-free. AAC is licensed, and MP3 was until 2017.

Proprietary

Some codecs belong to one company: XMA on Xbox, ATRAC9 on PlayStation, Bink Audio in Unreal. Each platform needs its own encode.

Hardware acceleration

Some platforms decode certain codecs on dedicated hardware, which frees CPU for more voices. XMA on Xbox is the usual example.

The basics

Four things that never show up as a number in the settings panel but can rule a codec out. Looping: perceptual codecs work on blocks of samples called frames, and the encoder adds a little delay and padding, so the decoded file can start or end with a sliver of silence. On a one-shot nobody notices; on a seamless loop it is a click or a gap. MP3 is the classic offender. Vorbis and Opus record how many samples to trim, and middleware normally handles it, but always audition a loop in the engine, not in the editor. ADPCM and PCM loop cleanly. Licensing: some codecs are covered by patents. Vorbis and Opus were designed to be royalty-free. MP3 needed a licence until its patents ran out in 2017. AAC is still patent-licensed; in practice games play it through the decoder the phone or console already ships with. [Verify the current AAC terms before saying more than that.] Proprietary: some codecs belong to one company. XMA is Microsoft's and runs on Xbox, ATRAC9 is Sony's and runs on PlayStation, and Bink Audio is Epic's, free inside Unreal. You get them through the platform SDK or the engine, and a multi-platform game ends up with a separate encode of every asset for each one. Hardware acceleration: some platforms decode certain codecs on dedicated hardware instead of the CPU. XMA on Xbox is the usual example, and most phones have a hardware AAC decoder. That frees CPU for more voices, which is the main reason to pick a platform codec.
