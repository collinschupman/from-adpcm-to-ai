The basics

10

A codec is a coder plus a decoder. For game assets the encode happens once, on a build machine, and the decode happens on the player's device every time the sound plays, which is why decode cost matters far more than encode cost. (Voice chat is the exception: it encodes live.) Then the three types, and the two numbers people actually ask about: how much smaller, and what it costs to play. Compression ratio is original size against compressed size; 4:1 means a quarter of the size. Lossless (FLAC) predicts each sample and stores the error exactly, so everything comes back; the ratio depends on the material, usually somewhere between 1.5:1 and 2:1. Lossy codecs come in two families. Predictive ones like ADPCM do the same prediction but store only a rough, 4-bit version of the error: a fixed 4:1 whatever the sound, a little noise, and almost no CPU to decode. Perceptual ones like Vorbis, Opus and AAC move to the frequency domain and spend bits according to what the ear can hear. Their ratio is a setting, not a property: 128 kbps stereo is 12:1 against the 1,536 kbps of PCM, and 64 kbps is 24:1. They cost the most CPU and add latency. [The FLAC range is a general figure; check it against your own material before quoting it.]
