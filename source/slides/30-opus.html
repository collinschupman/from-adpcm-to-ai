Opus

An open, royalty-free codec built for real-time voice that turned out to be good at everything.

Standard

IETF RFC 6716 (2012), royalty-free

How

SILK for speech, CELT for music, or a hybrid

Range

6 to 510 kbps; frames from 2.5 to 60 ms

Strength

Best quality per bit at low bitrates

Hardware

Decoded in hardware on Xbox Series X|S

Use it for

Dialogue, music, long streams, voice chat

Against Vorbis

Better quality at low bitrates.

Costs more CPU to decode: 500 to 1,300 streams against Vorbis's 2,300 to 5,700 in Audiokinetic's test.

Default new work to Opus. Keep Vorbis where a pipeline already depends on it.

The basics

30

Opus was built for real-time communication, which is why it is so strong on speech and at low bitrates: SILK handles speech, CELT handles music, and hybrid mode blends them. It covers 6 to 510 kbps with frames from 2.5 to 60 ms, it is an IETF standard (RFC 6716, 2012), and it is royalty-free. Xbox Series X|S decodes it in hardware. Against Vorbis: Opus is better at low bitrates but costs more CPU; Audiokinetic measured 500 to 1,300 Opus streams against 2,300 to 5,700 for Vorbis on the same PC. The honest framing: Opus is the better default for new work, but switching a shipped pipeline isn't free. The cost is decode CPU, which the CPU slide comes back to in a few minutes.
